Haven

quiet, tuned to you

Hearing protection that softens what bothers you and keeps the rest.

A small sealed earbud blocks loud sound, then plays the room back with the frequencies you choose turned down. You set them in a calm, guided app.

See it in the ear Follow the build

The problem

Earplugs make everything quieter, including what you came to hear.

Foam plugs dull voices and music along with the noise. Active earbuds are built for listening to music, not for protecting your hearing at a concert or on a job site.

Concerts and clubs

Front-of-house levels often pass 100 dB. You want the band clear and the harsh peaks gone.

Loud work

Shops, kitchens and stages mix tools with conversation. Blocking everything means missing the person next to you.

Sounds that grate

Some people find particular pitches uncomfortable. A flat earplug can't single them out.

How it works

Seal, listen, soften, play back.

Everything below happens inside an earbud about the size of a sugar cube. Turn the model, pull it apart, or step through the sound path.

  1. SealA foam tip blocks most outside sound, even with the power off.
  2. ListenA tiny outward mic picks up the room.
  3. SoftenThe audio chip turns down up to five frequency bands you choose and caps the output level.
  4. Play backA small speaker plays the result into the sealed ear, fast enough not to echo.

Live 3D model of the v2 design at true scale. The ear is built from published anatomical dimensions, not a scan of a real person.

The app

Calm controls, one job per screen.

The app is where you decide what Haven softens. It talks to the earbud over Bluetooth and never lets the sound go past a fixed ceiling.

Home

One glance tells you if you're protected or paused. A slow amber light, nothing to configure.

Tune

Pick up to five frequency bands and how much to soften each, from a gentle dip to fully removed.

Comfort check

Tones rise slowly from quiet so you can mark what feels uncomfortable. A large stop button is always in reach, and both the app and the earbud enforce an 85 dB ceiling.

Check-ins

Note how a day felt in a few taps, and see over weeks which settings worked for you.

Hardware

Small enough to forget, built on proven parts.

The v2 design packs a Bluetooth chip, an audio chip with its own signal processor, a mic, a speaker and a rechargeable cell into a body about 17 × 19 × 18 mm. These are design targets, not measured results yet.

Where Haven is right now

Haven is a prototype in development. Nothing is for sale. Here's the honest picture.

Built
App and firmware

Written and covered by automated tests. Bluetooth control and the safety limits run on a development kit.

Designed
v1 earbud board

Laid out, checked and quoted. Waiting for two parts to come back in stock.

In design
v2: smaller earbud and charging case

Circuit done. The shell is being reshaped to sit securely in real ears.

Not yet
Sound through Haven's own hardware

No audio has passed through a Haven board yet, and output levels haven't been measured. That comes with the first built boards.

Built in the open

Every file is public.

Haven builds on OpenEarable, an open-source research earbud. The app, firmware, circuit boards and enclosure are all on GitHub.
